Can a video decoder execute a program?

This page compiles a small computation into a standard H.264 video stream. After a few start-up frames, the stream repeats the same 16 instruction frames, and each decoded picture supplies the state for the next step. Change the input, and watch your browser's video decoder run it.

JavaScript builds and feeds the stream. The H.264 decoder updates the running state.

    What your decoder is fed
    
  
    JavaScript writes the first frames once: the starting state, as raw samples, then copies so that every delay in the program can read it. After that it only hands your decoder the 16 compiled program frames, over and over, through WebCodecs. The page keeps no copy of the state; it lives in the decoder's reference frames.

    How the decoder computes a generation
    
  
    H.264 builds a B-frame macroblock by copying blocks from earlier frames and mixing them with explicit weights: clip(((w0·a + w1·b + 2L) >> (L+1)) + o). Each field below is a 200 × 200 board in its own region of the frame, rewritten every frame by one such mix, and motion vectors choose the cells it reads. One generation takes 6 frames, so at 60 frames a second the board advances 10 times a second. Flat out, it advances as fast as your decoder decodes.

    How the decoder executes an instruction
    
  
    H.264 builds a B-frame macroblock by copying blocks from earlier frames and mixing them with explicit weights. With weights 2 and 2 the mix of two fields of 0s and 255s is clip(a + b), an OR; with 2 and −2 it is clip(a − b), a AND NOT b. Every field here is 16 × 64 cells: bit k of register r is cell (r, k), so the register bank is one field. The motion vectors are fixed, so the circuit cannot point at a register: it reads all 64 and keeps the one the instruction names, by masking its row and ORing the rows together with a butterfly of shifts. Rotating is reading one column over, because the board wraps round. The adder's carries run round the columns too, which gives ones' complement's end-around carry for free. The circuit is 334 fields, and its longest path is 17 frames, so one instruction takes 17 frames. The display is part of the circuit: it turns the word into decimal digits and draws their segments.

If your browser shows something else, that is a decoder finding worth reporting.
